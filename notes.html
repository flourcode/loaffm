Liner notes

Loafie, the five stations, the four looks, her sky, and how any of this works.

A1Meet Loafie

Loafie lives on the desk under the clock. She wanders, loafs, grooms, watches the weather and stares into space. Leave her alone long enough and she dozes off.

Pet her. Drop her a fish. Tap Z and she curls up for a nap and the board dims; touch the board and she's up again. She has a box. The rest you'll figure out.

A2Focus: five stations

Hit FOCUS on the tuner and step through them with ◀ ▶. Each has its own spot on the dial, and none of them is a recording. The music is written as it plays, so it never comes round the same way twice.

Late Shift
Warm Rhodes and a lazy beat. Built for working late.
Rainy Window
Soft keys, a little melody, rain on the glass. Study mode.
CRT Dreams
Analog pads, slow arpeggios, a few blips. The synthy one.
Sunday Loaf
Jazz chords, brushes and a purr underneath. Zero urgency.
Desert Night
Slow pads, far-off bells and crickets. Wind-down mode.

A3Calm

Hit CALM for purr, rain, ocean, fireplace or brown noise. The meter turns into a slow breathing light: four seconds in, six out. Follow it or don't. Tap Z and Loafie naps with you while the board dims.

A4Four looks

Tap the little display key to flip through them.

Pegs
The light-up peg toy you had as a kid. Goes with 8-bit.
Teal Stereo
A late-80s receiver with orange lights. Goes with House.
Amber Radio
The clock radio on the dresser. Goes with Lo-fi.
Moonlight
Icy blue, lavender, a little gold. Goes with Ambient.

Until you pick one, the display follows the track. Rain, fire, ocean and purr tint it a little, and after dark it dims on its own.

A5Her sky

Every station brings its own weather: rain on Rainy Window, stars and the odd meteor on Late Shift and Desert Night, falling green code on CRT Dreams, leaves on Sunday Loaf.

With nothing playing, it follows the real world: stars at night, snow in winter, petals in spring, fireflies on summer nights.

A6Leave it running

On a computer, the float key keeps Loafie on top of your other windows. You can still pet her and feed her there.

Got an old tablet? Add Loaf FM to the home screen, keep it plugged in, and you've got a desk clock with a cat in it.

Once it's loaded, it works offline.

A7Nerd stuff

Nothing here is an audio file. The drums, bass, keys, rain, waves, fire and purr are synthesised in your browser with the Web Audio API, note by note, while you listen.

It's procedural, not AI. Every melody note is pulled onto the chord underneath it, which is why any mix you stack sounds right.

A mixtape link is just a handful of choices and a seed. Your friend's browser plays the rest.

No keyboard shortcuts, on purpose: you're probably typing in another window, and a stray key shouldn't stop the music.

The whole thing is small, because a clock radio with a cat in it shouldn't need 40 MB of JavaScript.
